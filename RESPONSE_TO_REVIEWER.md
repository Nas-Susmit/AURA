# AURA — Response to major-revision review

**Revised title:** AURA: Assessing the Limits of Disagreement-Aware Selective Prediction in an Axial Spondyloarthritis-Inspired Synthetic Benchmark

We appreciate the detailed review. The revision now focuses on a negative methodological result rather than clinical diagnostic performance or a novel fusion architecture. We retained the original data generator, ten seeds, seven conditions and partitions and executed additional matched rankers, inferential comparisons, sensitivity analyses, diagnostics and attribution stability checks. The changes do not constitute external clinical validation. The following response is a draft for human authors to review and approve before submission.

## Major concerns

**1. Novelty and negative result.** Accepted. Title, abstract, introduction, discussion and conclusion now state that AURA is a lightweight test bed for a limits-of-methodological-claims study. We do not claim novelty for standard components or superiority over published methods. The main matched native disagreement effect is -0.000138 AURC (95% seed-level interval -0.000692 to +0.000415). No useful incremental effect is established; no equivalence or universal negative is claimed.

**2. Matched selective ranking.** Accepted. Sections IV-C, V-C and VI-B/C now distinguish common ranking protocols from within-classifier ablations. Early logistic/RF/HGB, equal/quality fusion and AURA all receive the same feature definitions (classifier-specific entropy; common equal-weight auxiliary expert variance and disagreement; shared mask and quality features), fitted separately on their own prediction errors using identical development observations and logistic ranker class. AURA retains its original native-weighted ablation as the primary RQ3 comparison. Twenty configurations yield 1,400 evaluations. Tables VI and VII separate across-classifier and within-classifier comparisons. AURA shared full AURC is 0.12191 versus early logistic 0.11998; the paired difference +0.00193 has an interval [-0.00073, +0.00459]. Claims are restricted accordingly. Shared auxiliary experts incur additional training cost.

**3. Inferential support for RQ1.** Accepted. Sections V-C and VI-A, Tables IV/V report AUROC, average precision (AUPRC), Brier and ECE, plus paired seed-level intervals for learned fusion versus both equal and quality fusion. AURA minus equal: AUROC +0.002506 [0.001995, 0.003016]; AUPRC +0.004857 [0.003811, 0.005904]; Brier -0.001498 [-0.001780, -0.001216]; ECE -0.003921 [-0.005512, -0.002329]. AURA minus quality: AUROC +0.002183 [0.001278, 0.003087]; AUPRC +0.004224 [0.002840, 0.005608]; Brier -0.001440 [-0.001887, -0.000992]; ECE -0.003512 [-0.005529, -0.001496]. These are small simulation-specific effects; secondary intervals are unadjusted. All other comparisons are available in revision_results/paired.csv.

**4. Risk80 ambiguity.** Accepted. Sections III, V-C, VI-D and Tables IX/X now distinguish R80 (select the lowest-scored floor(0.8n) test records; average exact ties) from conditional error at a frozen development cutoff (retain scores <= the development 80th percentile, report realized coverage). Complete: R80 0.13892 vs fixed cutoff coverage 0.88360 / risk 0.16085. Combined: R80 0.24400 vs fixed cutoff coverage 0.59560 / risk 0.20311. These are not the same operating point.

**5. Hyperparameters and disagreement importance.** Accepted. Sections V-C and VI-C, Table VIII report nine one-factor-at-a-time settings: B=3/5/10, clip=.01/.03/.10, ridge alpha=1/5/20, ranker C=.1/1/10. Identical observations and nested bootstrap members are used, with relevant models refitted. All nine paired disagreement intervals span zero. Mean effects range -0.000313 to +0.000029. The standardized coefficient of 4D averages 0.01551 (95% interval [-0.05819,0.08921]), with 5/10 positive; mean per-seed odds ratio 1.02060. Ten held-out marginal permutations per seed-condition increase AURC by 0.000436 [-0.000016,0.000887], and reduce error AUROC by 0.000948 [-0.000384,0.002279]. No reliable incremental importance is established. The checks cannot rule out interactions or establish equivalence. Full results are supplied, including per-seed coefficients.

**6. Clinical framing.** Accepted. All results concern the illustrative axSpA-inspired generator, not patient evidence structures or clinical readiness. The title, abstract and conclusion explicitly reflect this scope. Quality is an observed simulator variable; no coefficients are fit to patient distributions or likelihood ratios. The simulated binary outcome does not support differential diagnosis, and zero-input clinical policy is not evaluated.

**7. References and presentation.** Accepted. The original source and PDF contain 29 reference entries, although extraction appears to have missed them in the review copy. The revised compiled PDF retains all 29 entries; we checked the extracted final PDF and supplemented several bibliographic records. Eq. (6) is typeset as squared error (p_k - Y)^2; the original LaTeX was correct. HLA-B27 generator intercept is explicitly typeset -2.2, verified against code. Figure 3 now uses the Table III labels. Tables IV and VI have actual bold column minima/maxima as appropriate, generated from the data. The acknowledgment names ChatGPT (GPT-6.2 as identified for this revision; internal deployment snapshot not independently verified), specifies substantive AI use in text, code, figures and tables, and assigns full responsibility to human authors. The IEEE disclosure policy was checked at https://open.ieee.org/author-guidelines-for-artificial-intelligence-ai-generated-text/. Human authors must confirm disclosure, citations and findings before submission; an independent full-text reference audit is still recommended.

**8. Explanation evidence.** Accepted. The index terms and discussion now refer to attribution stability rather than clinical explainability. Section VI-E reports a new stability test across ten independently trained models and 150 common, independently generated, complete-condition anchors. Across 45 dependent seed pairs, mean modality-attribution absolute difference is 0.02143, sign agreement 97.15%, top-modality agreement 90.21%. First-member feature top-agreement is 66.07% clinical, 91.29% laboratory, 90.10% imaging; mean absolute logit attribution differences are .04787, .05452, .05480. Exact additivity is retained. This establishes limited fixed-input descriptive stability, not causal fidelity, clinical usefulness or complete ensemble explanation.

## Minor comments

- **AURC:** Section III specifies every retained-set size, tie-block expected cumulative errors and exact fixed-coverage risk. Fig. 2 samples 0.10–1.00 in 0.05 increments; AURC uses all ranks. Pointwise seed-level 95% confidence bands are shown.
- **Expert performance:** Table XI reports AUROC, average precision, Brier and ECE on available records, including raw and separately calibrated values. Availability cohorts differ; no direct controlled modality ranking is asserted.
- **Quality intervention:** Section V-B states that lower q changes both the observed quality score and the feature corruption process through the same noise equations.
- **Repair:** Section V-B explains forced nonempty masks as a conditional simulator design, not a realistic deferral policy. Nominal dropout differs from realized dropout.
- **Hyperparameters:** We added the sensitivity analysis above without test-set model selection. The analysis is review-motivated and exploratory.
- **Redundancy:** The interpretation of entropy, quality and disagreement redundancy remains explicitly a hypothesis, not an established mechanism.
